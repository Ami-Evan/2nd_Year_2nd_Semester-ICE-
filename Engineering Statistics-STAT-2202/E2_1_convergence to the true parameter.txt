## Experiment E2-1: Demonstrate Consistency by Increasing Sample Size

### Theory

Consistency is an important property of an estimator. An estimator is called **consistent** if its value gets closer to the true population parameter as the sample size increases.

In this experiment, samples are generated from a normal distribution with true mean **μ = 5**. The sample mean is calculated for different sample sizes from **10 to 1000**. As the sample size increases, the sample mean generally gets closer to the true mean 5. This demonstrates the **consistency of the sample mean estimator**.

### Objectives

1. To understand the concept of consistency of an estimator.
2. To generate random samples with a known population mean.
3. To calculate the sample mean for different sample sizes.
4. To observe how the sample mean approaches the true mean as sample size increases.
5. To visualize the convergence using a graph.

### Pseudocode

```text
START

Set true mean μ = 5

Set sample sizes from 10 to 1000
with an interval of 10

For each sample size n:
    Generate n random values
    from Normal distribution with mean 5 and SD 2

    Calculate the sample mean

    Store the sample mean

Display:
    Sample mean at n = 10
    Sample mean at n = 1000
    True mean

Create a graph:
    X-axis → Sample Size
    Y-axis → Sample Mean

Plot sample means
Draw a horizontal line at true mean μ = 5

Add title, labels and legend

Save the graph as a PDF file

END
```
