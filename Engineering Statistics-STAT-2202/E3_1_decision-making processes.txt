
### Experiment Name

**Simulate Decision-Making Process Using Hypothesis Testing**

### Theory

Hypothesis testing is a statistical method used to make a decision about a population based on sample data.

There are two hypotheses:

* **Null Hypothesis (H₀):** The population mean is 5, i.e., μ = 5.
* **Alternative Hypothesis (H₁):** The population mean is not 5, i.e., μ ≠ 5.

In this experiment, a sample of 30 observations is generated from a normal distribution with mean 5.5 and standard deviation 2. A **one-sample t-test** is performed to test whether the population mean is significantly different from 5.

The **p-value** is used to make the decision. At a 5% significance level:

* If **p-value < 0.05**, reject H₀.
* If **p-value ≥ 0.05**, fail to reject H₀.

A histogram is also plotted to visualize the sample data, the hypothesized mean, and the sample mean.

### Objectives

1. To understand the concept of hypothesis testing.
2. To generate sample data from a normal distribution.
3. To perform a one-sample t-test.
4. To calculate the test statistic and p-value.
5. To make a statistical decision using a 5% significance level.
6. To visualize the sample data using a histogram.

### Pseudocode

**START**

1. Set the random seed.
2. Generate 30 random observations from a normal distribution with mean 5.5 and standard deviation 2.
3. Set the null hypothesis H₀: μ = 5.
4. Set the alternative hypothesis H₁: μ ≠ 5.
5. Perform a one-sample t-test.
6. Calculate the test statistic and p-value.
7. If p-value < 0.05:

   * Reject H₀.
8. Otherwise:

   * Fail to reject H₀.
9. Display the test statistic, p-value, and decision.
10. Draw a histogram of the sample data.
11. Draw a vertical line at μ = 5.
12. Draw another vertical line at the sample mean.
13. Display the graph with a legend.

**END**
