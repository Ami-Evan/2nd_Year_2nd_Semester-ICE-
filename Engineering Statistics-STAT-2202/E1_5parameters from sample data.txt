## Objective

1. To generate sample data from a Normal distribution.
2. To estimate the population mean and variance from the sample.
3. To calculate the 95% confidence interval for the population mean.
4. To calculate the 95% confidence interval for the population variance.
5. To visualize the sample data using a histogram and theoretical Normal curve.

## Theory

Population parameters describe the characteristics of an entire population. The **population mean (μ)** represents the average value, while the **population variance (σ²)** represents the spread of the data.

In this experiment, a sample of **30 observations** is generated from a Normal distribution with mean **5** and standard deviation **2**. The sample mean and sample variance are used to estimate the population parameters.

For the confidence interval of the population mean, the **t-distribution** is used because the population variance is unknown.

For the confidence interval of the population variance, the **Chi-square distribution** is used with **n − 1 degrees of freedom**.

A histogram is used to display the distribution of the sample data. The theoretical Normal curve is added to compare the sample distribution with the expected population distribution. A vertical line represents the calculated sample mean.

## Pseudocode

**START**

1. Set the random seed to 123.
2. Set sample size `n = 30`.
3. Generate 30 random observations from Normal distribution with mean 5 and standard deviation 2.
4. Calculate the sample mean.
5. Calculate the sample variance.
6. Calculate the 95% confidence interval for the mean using the t-distribution.
7. Calculate the 95% confidence interval for the variance using the Chi-square distribution.
8. Display the sample mean, sample variance, and confidence intervals.
9. Plot a histogram of the sample data.
10. Add the theoretical Normal distribution curve.
11. Add a vertical line representing the sample mean.
12. Display the graph.

**END**
