## Experiment E2-3: Calculate Efficiency of Estimators — Mean vs Median

### Theory

Estimator efficiency refers to how precisely an estimator estimates an unknown population parameter. For two unbiased estimators of the same parameter, the estimator with **smaller variance** is considered more efficient.

In this experiment, the **sample mean** and **sample median** are used to estimate the population mean. Random samples are generated from a normal distribution with mean **μ = 5** and standard deviation **2**. The simulation is repeated 1000 times.

The variances of the sample means and medians are then compared. The relative efficiency is calculated using the ratio of the variance of the median to the variance of the mean.

### Objectives

1. To understand the concept of estimator efficiency.
2. To compare the efficiency of the sample mean and sample median.
3. To calculate the variance of both estimators.
4. To calculate the relative efficiency of the median with respect to the mean.
5. To visualize the distributions of the two estimators using a boxplot.

### Pseudocode

```text
START

Set sample size n = 30
Set number of simulations = 1000
Set true mean μ = 5

Create arrays for Mean and Median estimates

FOR i = 1 to 1000

    Generate a random sample of size 30
    from Normal distribution with mean 5
    and standard deviation 2

    Calculate sample mean
    Calculate sample median

    Store both values

END FOR

Calculate variance of all sample means

Calculate variance of all sample medians

Calculate relative efficiency:
    Variance of Median / Variance of Mean

Display:
    Variance of Sample Mean
    Variance of Sample Median
    Relative Efficiency

Create a boxplot comparing Mean and Median

Draw a horizontal line at true mean = 5

END
```
