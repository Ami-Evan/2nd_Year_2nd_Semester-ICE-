## Experiment E3-2: Derive the Best Critical Region for Simple vs Composite Hypotheses

### Theory

A **critical region** is the range of sample values for which the null hypothesis \(H_0\) is rejected. In this experiment, we consider:

* \(H_0: \mu = 0\)
* \(H_1: \mu = 2\)
* Standard deviation, \(\sigma = 1\)
* Significance level, \(\alpha = 0.05\)

Since the alternative hypothesis has a larger mean, a **right-tailed test** is used. The critical value is obtained from the standard normal distribution. At \(\alpha=0.05\), the critical value is approximately **1.645**.

Therefore, the best critical region is:

$$
\bar{x} > 1.645
$$

If the sample mean is greater than 1.645, we reject \(H_0\).

### Objectives

1. To understand the concept of a critical region.
2. To determine the critical value for a given significance level.
3. To compare the distributions under \(H_0\) and \(H_1\).
4. To visualize the critical region using a graph.
5. To understand when the null hypothesis should be rejected.

### Pseudocode

```text

    H0: mu = 0

    sigma = 1

    critical_value = qnorm(1 - alpha)
```
