### Objective

1. To generate random samples from a normal population.
2. To calculate the median and range of each sample.
3. To study the distribution of sample medians and sample ranges.
4. To observe the variation of medians and ranges across many samples.

### Theory

Sampling is the process of selecting observations from a population. When repeated random samples are taken from a population, statistics calculated from those samples also form distributions. These are called **sampling distributions**.

In this experiment, samples of size **20** are generated from a normal population with mean **50** and standard deviation **10**. A total of **1000 samples** are generated.

For each sample, two statistics are calculated:

* **Median:** The middle value of the ordered sample.
* **Range:** The difference between the maximum and minimum values of the sample.

The 1000 sample medians and 1000 sample ranges are then displayed using histograms. These histograms help us understand how the sample median and range vary from one sample to another.

### Pseudocode

```text
START

Set random seed = 123

Set sample size n = 20

Set number of simulations = 1000

Create arrays for medians and ranges

FOR i = 1 to 1000

    Generate a random sample of 20 values
    from Normal distribution with mean = 50
    and standard deviation = 10

    Calculate the median of the sample
    Store the median

    Calculate:
        Range = Maximum value - Minimum value

    Store the range

END FOR

Calculate the mean of all sample medians

Calculate the mean of all sample ranges

Display the calculated means

Plot histogram of sample medians

Plot histogram of sample ranges

Save the graphs in a PDF file

END
```
